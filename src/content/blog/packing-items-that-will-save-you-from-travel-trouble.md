Travelers frequently overpack while simultaneously forgetting crucial items they didn't anticipate needing. Based on personal experience visiting over 30 countries, I've compiled a curated list of essential packing items for any journey.

## Printouts of Tickets

Keep physical copies of all advance bookings—attractions, transportation, and accommodations—in an easily accessible itinerary book. "Sometimes technology fails. You don't want to be about to board a plane when your phone can't be scanned" due to technical issues. Paper backups have proven invaluable when digital records weren't available.

## Medicine Kit

Travel affects the body unpredictably, and medication availability varies internationally. Assembling a personalized medicine kit is essential, with smaller versions for day packs. The CDC provides guidance on recommended items, though contents should reflect your specific trip needs. Note that certain medications are restricted in some countries.

## Packing Cubes

These organizational tools compress items while maintaining order, eliminating messy bag searches. They also expedite security screenings, keeping personal items private from public view.

## Itinerary Book

Maintain a dedicated journal detailing daily activities, tickets, addresses, transportation directions, and site information. Front pages should contain emergency contacts, travel insurance details, and embassy addresses. Back pages can hold passport and ID photocopies for theft scenarios. This preparation eliminates daily planning stress.

## Empty Water Bottle

Carry a reusable bottle for destinations with safe tap water. Fill it after airport security to avoid expensive retail purchases and reduce plastic consumption. This alone saves approximately $10 per trip.

## Backups of Every Important Document

Create multiple color copies of critical documents:
- State identification
- Passport identity pages (multiple copies recommended)
- Passport photographs
- Medical records
- Visas
- Travel insurance documents
- Coded credit card information
- Travel itinerary
- Marriage certificates (sometimes required for hotel accommodations)

Share copies with a trusted contact for emergency access.

## Multiple Battery Packs for Phones

Since phones serve numerous functions during travel and charging opportunities are limited, carrying multiple quality portable chargers is practical. Ensure they're fully charged each evening.

## Ponchos

Despite unflattering appearances, ponchos provide superior weather protection compared to umbrellas. They keep hands free and pack compactly into day bags.

## Money Belt

Hidden zipper pouches worn around the neck, ankle, or waist provide discrete money storage. Discretion is paramount—retrievals should occur in private restrooms, never publicly, as obvious transactions invite theft. Thieves target convenience.

## Ear Plugs and Eye Mask

Sleep quality during travel varies greatly. These items counteract streetlights, noise from infants on flights, and other disturbances. Test them before departure.

## Portable Power Strip with USB Plugs

Many accommodations offer limited outlets. A compact power strip accommodates multiple device charging simultaneously.

## Plug Adapter

Different countries feature different electrical outlets and voltage specifications. An all-in-one international adapter enables device charging across regions. Carrying multiple adapters provides backup options. Avoid American hair styling tools unless they feature dual voltage capability; regular appliances can overload foreign electrical systems and sustain damage.

## Ziplock Baggies

Sandwich and gallon-sized bags protect belongings during rain and organize day pack contents.

## Water Resistant Shoes/Jacket

Precipitation is inevitable. Water-resistant footwear and outerwear maintain comfort during unexpected weather.

## Emergency Cash

Credit card failures due to loss or compromise leave travelers vulnerable. Maintaining a week's worth of emergency currency provides essential financial cushioning during card replacement delays.

## Heel Covers/Mole Skin

Daily walking during travel averages approximately 10 miles. Moleskin manages blisters and skin irritations from extended walking.

## Extra Passport Photos

Visa-on-arrival destinations and official documentation require passport photographs. Shortage results in expensive fees and extended waits. Research specific country requirements beforehand, as photo specifications vary by nation regarding dimensions and background colors.
